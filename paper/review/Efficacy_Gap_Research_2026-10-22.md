## Finding that changes the experiment

The controlled provider currently returns exact evidence quotations and withholds practice/continue recommendations. Its release policy can reject fabricated quotations, review a draft subject reference, and route selected lexical signals. It cannot generally decide whether the student's quoted technical statement is true, whether a quotation is misleading out of context, or whether equivalent students receive equivalent pedagogical feedback. P2 and P9 in `output/chunk28/current-policy-probes.json` demonstrate two such misses. The keyword score is only an authority-test fixture.

Therefore **do not call a quotation-only run a general hallucination-reduction experiment**. Its measurable outcome is source-provenance enforcement and the cost of withholding/review. A claim about technical-feedback hallucinations requires an implemented feedback-claim contract, a faculty-approved technical source/rubric, and independent claim-level judgments.

## Two-stage study, with a hard evidence boundary

### Stage A — current-policy characterization

The executable asset today is `TrustInterview-AI/backend/research/evaluation/characterize-current-policy.cjs`. It calls the exported `outputPolicy` function for nine authored inputs and writes `output/chunk28/current-policy-probes.json`. That report identifies policy `research-0.8`, records no live model calls or independent human labels, and records four `allow`, three `review` and two `abstain` decisions. The backend JUnit report contains 91 passing tests, including the copied-bearer-token negative case documented in `output/chunk30/ZTA_Negative_Cases.md`. These are the **only current Stage A results**. The probe expectations were authored with the cases, so agreement between expected and observed decisions is a conformance check, not accuracy.

A matched replay against an ungated recording condition is a **proposed experiment, not an existing implementation or result**. It would require a frozen input set, captured provider proposals, an explicit baseline definition and identical proposals across conditions. Keep the access policy on in both conditions because candidate isolation is not an ablation to expose in a shared deployment. New, held-out cases would require the planned protocol and faculty review before collection.

Possible later Stage A outcomes are fabricated-quote releases, technically false but literal quote releases, release/review/abstain counts and reviewer workload. Access-control checks must be reported separately from output-policy checks. Report exact denominators and examples. The nine authored probes cannot yield a population detection rate and must stay outside a future held-out test set.

### Stage B — required for a stronger empirical claim

Add a separate, explicitly versioned **feedback-claim mode** after Stage A is frozen. A generated observation should include an atomic claim, its type (`student_attribution`, `technical_explanation`, or `mastery_judgment`), source identifier(s), evidence span(s), and a proposed decision. Do not let the provider assign an authoritative score. Keep the current quotation-only mode as a safe comparator. Faculty supply a versioned technical answer key, scope notes and a rubric for each question. An administrative `faculty_reviewed` flag alone is insufficient: the reviewed source needs recorded content and version hashes.

The controlled service currently uses one fixed ZTA question. An empirical study could begin with that question, but its result would be limited to that question. Additional questions, answer scenarios and a held-out size must be chosen with faculty before implementation and collection; no number is assumed here. Exclude examples used to develop prompts or rules from the eventual held-out set.

For a future comparison, record a frozen model/provider version and prompt per configuration. Capture raw proposed feedback before gating and replay the same proposal through clearly defined conditions, including the current quote gate and any new faculty-source semantic gate. Such conditions and a repeated-generation run do not yet exist. Do not present their effects until implementation, collection and validation are complete.

## Annotation and outcome definitions

Two independent reviewers, blinded to configuration and output decision, label each **atomic feedback claim** `supported`, `contradicted`, or `undetermined` against (a) the student's answer for attribution and (b) the faculty key for technical truth. They mark an exact evidence span and whether a technically true quotation is used misleadingly. Record original labels before third-person adjudication. Report raw agreement, label distributions, unresolved cases and adjudicated outcomes. A model-based judge may be an auxiliary comparator, never the sole gold label.

The primary grounding outcome is the number of **unsupported or contradicted generated claims released to a student per 100 generated claims**, with both numerator and denominator shown. Report undetermined separately. Secondary outcomes are claim-level precision/recall or macro-F1 against adjudicated labels, extraction coverage, abstention and review rates, and the proportion of fully supported outputs among released outputs. A rule that withholds nearly everything can reduce released errors; therefore always report coverage and the review burden alongside error rates. A technically false statement from the student quoted as *student evidence* is distinct from the bot endorsing it as a true explanation; annotations must preserve this distinction.

Bias is assessed with **faculty-validated meaning-preserving pairs** of the same technical answer. Vary one task-irrelevant presentation cue at a time; first test name/pronoun presentation and language-style variants only when faculty confirms that technical meaning, clarity requirements and scoring criteria are held constant. Avoid making a personal disclosure the sole fairness test: the present lexical screen intentionally routes such a quote to review, so this measures review burden rather than discriminatory grading. Keep demographic inference from names out of the bot's decision logic. The pair is the analysis unit: compare within-pair release, review, abstention and claim-quality outcomes, then report the direction and size of differences with uncertainty. Multiple model samples should be clustered by pair rather than counted as independent students.

Security and Zero Trust remain a separate outcome family: unauthorized access and forbidden state transitions are counted as failures even if a warning is emitted. Do not combine the access and grounding metrics into a single success percentage. Log latency, provider errors and reviewer time for each condition. Predefine primary outcomes and analysis choices before seeing held-out results; a small pilot should report uncertainty and avoid claims of population fairness.

## Existing assets and what must change

The current `output/chunk15` 48-case pilot and `output/chunk17` offline run are procedure checks. Their role/job-text pairs use a fixed ZTA question and unchanged keyword score, so they cannot measure model-driven interview grading fairness. The existing `annotation_tools.py` supports three-way source labels, evidence spans, agreement and descriptive metrics; its `paired_input` labels concern evidence sufficiency, not fairness of generated feedback. Extend the schema only after the new research question and feedback contract are frozen, and preserve the old artifacts/version for traceability.

To execute Stage B, the project needs: (1) faculty-reviewed question/key/rubric versions; (2) a claim-output contract and semantic gate that distinguishes attribution from technical truth; (3) raw proposal capture plus matched replay; (4) blinded annotation packets and adjudication; (5) paired-output analysis with coverage, misses and burden; and (6) a frozen result export with source, input, prompt, model and policy hashes. Any student data collection must follow the institution's applicable approval and privacy procedure. None of these requirements is satisfied by a passing unit-test count.

## Submission consequence

The current manuscript is accurately scoped as an architecture/prototype paper. It can be submitted under that scope. A revised *empirical* paper would replace part of the proposed-study section with Stage A and B outcomes only after collection. Until then, the phrases “bias mitigation demonstrated,” “hallucinations reduced,” and “fair scoring” would exceed the evidence.
